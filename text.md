Digital technologies are often perceived as immaterial, yet they depend on natural resources (media-1), physical infrastructures (media-2), and human labour (media-3) (■ Parikka, 2015. A geology of media). Artificial intelligence builds upon these foundations, introducing additional demands through large-scale computation, extensive data processing, and increasingly complex models (■ Crawford, 2021. Atlas of AI.). These activities consume electricity, require cooling systems that may use substantial amounts of water (media-4), and contribute to environmental impacts (media-5) across hardware production (media-6), operation, and disposal (■ Aczel et al., 2026. Environmental cost of AI’s energy use: Carbon, water and land footprints). Investigating these material dependencies involves two complementary approaches: {crimson|revealing} the resources (media-7) (media-8), infrastructures (media-8) and labour behind AI systems, and {green|tracking} (media-9) (media-10) their material traces across extraction, production, use, and disposal. Both approaches can be applied across four interconnected dimensions, each opening different questions about AI’s materialities: 1) Geology and ecology {crimson|✶ What does AI extract and consume?} Minerals (media-11), land (media-12), energy, and water, with uneven environmental impacts across territories (■ Nicoletti et al., 2025. The AI Boom Is Draining Water From the Areas That Need It Most). 2) Infrastructure and computation {crimson|✶ Where and how is AI computation performed?} Data centres, factories, cables, and processors reveal its geographical organisation (media-13) (■ Coletta et al., 2022. Mapping the clouds: the matter of data centers.). 3) Human labour {crimson|✶ Whose work makes AI possible?} (media-14) Mining, manufacturing, data annotation (media-15) (media-16), moderation, maintenance, and disposal sustain automated systems (■ Gray & Suri, 2019. Ghost work: How to stop Silicon Valley from building a new global underclass). 4) Embodiment {crimson|✶ How does AI interact with bodies and environments?} Sensors, cameras (media-17), wearables, robots (media-18), and connected objects embed algorithmic processes into everyday life (■ Greenfield, 2017. Radical technologies: The design of everyday life.). Each investigation focuses on a specific aspect of AI’s materialities, explored through the collection and analysis of data. The findings are presented through a data visualisation and a multimodal abstract designed to present the topic and expand its interpretation.
